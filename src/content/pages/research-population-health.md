## Map exercises and categories

The Population Health Center at fictional Alder Coast University maintains invented map exercises for teaching how categories and legends shape a public-information display. The maps use aggregate symbols and fictional place names rather than observations about real residents or neighborhoods. A catalogue entry identifies the map's topic, the labels in its legend and the intended classroom discussion.

## Questions about access

One exercise compares two versions of a public-information map: one organized by subject and another by document format. Learners note which labels make a reading collection easier to find and which distinctions disappear when categories are combined. The exercise concerns information access and map design, not a measured public-health outcome.

The center connects academic study with the university's public-health program and its fictional partnerships page. It records no real datasets, recruitment, research findings or clinical claims; the examples exist solely to illustrate how aggregate materials can be catalogued.
