# Sleep Center Demonstration Rooms

Fictional demonstration website. Not a real healthcare provider.

## An orientation to teaching spaces

This room guide maps an invented set of spaces used for classroom demonstrations at the fictional Sleep Center. It describes room labels and display objects so a reader can picture the teaching environment. It does not describe a real clinic, offer visit preparation advice or indicate that a service is available.

## Demonstration bedroom display

The demonstration bedroom contains a wall diagram of a mock room, a small acoustic panel sample and a card explaining how designers record the features of a teaching space. The diagram uses simple shapes and invented measurements. Its caption separates the objects shown in the display from any claim about how a real room is built or used.

## Observation room

The observation room is presented as a seminar room with an instructor desk, a screen for diagrams and a shelf of sample recording forms. The forms are blank teaching props. A label describes their column headings as examples for a document-reading exercise, rather than instructions for collecting information about a person.

## Orientation room

The orientation room groups three tabletop objects: a scale room model, a set of sound-dampening material samples and a printed floor-plan key. Each object has a short catalogue card with an item name, a display category and a fictional inventory number. The cards show how a collection can be organized around shared vocabulary.

## Reading the room labels

Students can compare the room names in this guide with the captions on a classroom diagram. The labels distinguish a room's teaching purpose from the objects placed there. In a search exercise, this difference helps demonstrate how a document can discuss sleep-related education without presenting clinical advice or claiming patient outcomes. All spaces and materials in this guide are invented for a public demonstration corpus.
