# Research Document Vocabulary

Fictional demonstration website. Not a real healthcare provider.

## Labels in a fictional information set

This guide explains the headings used in an invented set of research-information documents. It is a vocabulary reference for a classroom exercise, not a study invitation, recruitment notice, consent form or description of a real project. The fictional examples help readers distinguish documents by purpose and audience.

## Information sheet

An information sheet is a document label for background material in this teaching collection. The sample heading includes a topic, a version marker and a plain-language summary line. A margin key identifies where the title and summary appear. The example does not describe a real research activity or ask anyone to take part.

## Study record

The phrase study record labels an archival-style example that stores administrative descriptions of an invented project. Its catalogue card lists a record title, a fictional department name and a date used only for classroom organization. The record is kept separate from explanatory material so students can compare a reference document with a stored administrative entry.

## Glossary and index

The glossary defines common document terms such as version, summary, record and archive in the context of this sample set. The index groups entries by document role rather than by a real research topic. Students can follow an index term to a document heading and note whether both use the same wording.

## Reading the distinction

The information sheet explains a subject in the fictional collection, while the study record describes how one invented item is catalogued. The glossary and index help readers recognize those roles. There is no invitation, eligibility criterion, consent language, real investigator or contact route in the example. Every title and organizational detail belongs to the fictional Alder Coast University Health demonstration.
